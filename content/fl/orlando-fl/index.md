## 1. Immediate Steps After a Crash in Orlando, Florida

### Ensure Safety and Call for Help

The moments following a car accident can be disorienting, but prioritizing safety is paramount. First, check yourself and your passengers for injuries. If possible, move your vehicle to a safe location, such as the shoulder of the road, especially if you're on a busy highway like Interstate 4 (I-4) or the Florida Turnpike.

Once you're in a safe spot, call 911 to report the accident. This is crucial, as having an official police report can be instrumental when dealing with insurance claims and potential legal proceedings. The Orlando Police Department and Florida Highway Patrol are the primary agencies that will respond, depending on the exact location of the crash.

- Check for injuries and ensure everyone’s safety.

- Move the vehicle to a safe location if possible.

- Call 911 to report the accident.


### Exchange Information and Document the Scene

After ensuring everyone's safety and contacting emergency services, the next step is to exchange information with the other driver(s) involved. This includes names, contact details, insurance information, and vehicle registration numbers. It's also wise to note the make, model, and color of each vehicle involved.

Documenting the scene is equally important. Use your phone to take pictures of the vehicles, any visible injuries, and the surrounding area. This visual evidence can be invaluable when piecing together the events leading up to the crash, especially if you decide to consult a licensed attorney in Florida for further advice.

- Exchange names, contact details, and insurance information.

- Take photos of the vehicles, injuries, and accident scene.

- Note the make, model, and color of all vehicles involved.

## 3. How to Deal with Insurance Companies After a Orlando Crash

### Understanding Florida's No-Fault Insurance System

Florida operates under a no-fault insurance system, which means that your own insurance company is responsible for covering your medical expenses and lost wages, regardless of who was at fault in the accident. This system is designed to streamline the claims process and reduce the need for litigation.

Every driver in Florida is required to carry Personal Injury Protection (PIP) insurance with a minimum coverage of $10,000. PIP covers 80% of necessary medical expenses and 60% of lost wages, up to the policy limits. Additionally, drivers must have Property Damage Liability (PDL) coverage with a minimum of $10,000 to cover damage to another person's property.

- Florida's no-fault insurance covers medical expenses and lost wages.

- PIP insurance minimum coverage is $10,000.

- PDL insurance minimum coverage is $10,000.


### Filing a Claim with Your Insurance

After a car accident in Orlando, you should notify your insurance company as soon as possible. Provide them with accurate details of the accident and any documentation you've gathered, such as photos and the police report.

While dealing with insurance companies, it's crucial to understand your rights and responsibilities under Florida's insurance laws. If you're unsure about any aspect of the claims process, it may be beneficial to consult a licensed attorney in Florida who can offer guidance tailored to your situation.

- Notify your insurance company promptly.

- Provide accurate accident details and documentation.

- Consider consulting a licensed attorney for guidance.


## 4. Common Injuries & What to Expect

### Typical Injuries from Car Accidents

Car accidents can result in a wide range of injuries, from minor cuts and bruises to more serious conditions like whiplash, fractures, or concussions. It's important to seek medical attention immediately, even if your injuries seem minor at first.

In Orlando, several hospitals such as Orlando Regional Medical Center and AdventHealth Orlando offer comprehensive emergency services and can provide the necessary medical care following an accident. Remember, some injuries might not be immediately apparent, so a thorough medical evaluation is crucial.

- Common injuries include whiplash, fractures, and concussions.

- Seek immediate medical attention, even for minor symptoms.

- Visit local hospitals like Orlando Regional Medical Center for care.

This documentation not only supports your insurance claims but also helps establish the extent of your injuries if you decide to pursue legal action. Always consult a licensed attorney in Florida to understand how best to document and present your medical evidence.
