## 1. Immediate Steps After a Crash in Austin, Texas

The first ten minutes after a collision set the tone for your safety, your medical recovery, and the quality of evidence available later. Austin-area crashes range from low-speed parking-lot taps on South Congress to high-speed chain reactions on SH 130. The principles are the same; the risk profile is not.

### 1.1 Safety First: Protect Yourself From a Second Collision

Texas roads do not stop for your accident. On **I-35** through Central Austin, **MoPac**, or the **US 183** corridor in North Austin, standing in a live lane is one of the most dangerous choices you can make. If your vehicle can be moved safely and you are not seriously injured, Texas practice and Austin Police guidance generally favor getting out of travel lanes when possible—turn on hazard flashers, move to the shoulder or a frontage road, and stay visible.

If you cannot move the vehicle, keep seatbelts fastened until traffic is controlled or law enforcement arrives, especially when vehicles are passing at highway speed. Use cones or flares only if you can deploy them without entering a dangerous lane. On frontage roads along I-35, secondary crashes are common when drivers rubberneck; increasing following distance for others may not be possible, which is why **early hazard lights** and **early 911** matter.

For crashes involving pedestrians or cyclists near downtown, the Warehouse District, or East Austin arterials, prioritize calling **911** immediately. Do not assume “no visible damage” means no injury—pedestrian and bicycle collisions often produce delayed internal trauma.

### 1.2 Call 911 and Request Austin Police or Appropriate Agency

Dial **911** when anyone reports injury, when airbags deploy, when vehicles block lanes, when you suspect impairment, or when the other party is hostile or leaves the scene. In Austin city limits, **Austin Police Department (APD)** may respond; on some highways, **Texas Department of Public Safety (DPS)** troopers respond instead. Outside city limits in Travis County, **Travis County Sheriff’s Office** may handle the scene.

When dispatch answers, state your location as precisely as possible: highway name, direction of travel, nearest exit or cross street, and landmarks (“northbound I-35 near Airport Blvd,” “MoPac southbound before Lake Austin Blvd”). If you are on a frontage road, say so—responders route differently than for mainlanes.

Cooperate with officers, but avoid arguing fault at the scene. You can provide facts—“the light was green,” “I was stopped in traffic”—without delivering a lengthy narrative. Request the **officer’s name, badge number, and report number** before they leave. If police cannot respond due to volume (common during severe weather or major events like SXSW, ACL Festival, or UT football Saturdays), ask dispatch how to file a report and document that you attempted to report.

### 1.3 Document the Scene Like an Investigator (Calmly)

Your smartphone is your best tool. Once safe, photograph:

- All four sides of every vehicle involved, including license plates
- Damage close-ups and wide shots showing position in the roadway
- Skid marks, debris, and road conditions (wet leaves after Austin storms, sun glare at dusk, construction barrels)
- Traffic controls—signals, stop signs, temporary construction signage
- Cross streets, exit numbers, and highway signs (especially on 290 and 183)

Video a slow pan of the scene if traffic allows. Capture **weather and lighting**—Austin’s sudden downpours and low sun on westbound evening commutes are frequent contributing factors insurers later debate.

Exchange information with other drivers: full name, phone, address, driver’s license number, insurance company and policy number, and vehicle registration. If the other driver refuses, note their plate and vehicle description and inform police. For **Uber/Lyft** collisions, screenshot the trip in the app; rideshare claims involve additional coverage layers.

Identify witnesses. Ask for name and phone number. Bystanders on Lamar, Burnet Road, or Slaughter Lane often leave before police arrive—politely ask if they will wait two minutes or text you their contact.

### 1.4 Medical Check: Do Not “Tough It Out” Texas-Style

Adrenaline masks injury. Accept EMS evaluation if offered. If EMS is not dispatched, plan a **same-day medical visit** if you have any neck soreness, headache, dizziness, numbness, abdominal pain, or confusion—even if airbags did not deploy.

Tell medical providers the crash date and that symptoms began after a motor vehicle collision. Consistent medical records help later, whether you pursue an insurance claim or litigation. Keep every discharge summary, imaging order, prescription, and bill.

### 1.5 Notify Insurance—Carefully and in Writing When Possible

Texas policies typically require **prompt notice** of a potential claim. You should report the crash, but you are not required to give a recorded statement immediately while shaken. Many Austin residents call their insurer from the scene; if you do, stick to basics: date, location, vehicles involved, and that you are seeking medical evaluation.

Avoid phrases that sound like admissions—“I wasn’t paying attention,” “I guess I was in a hurry”—even if you are being polite. Insurers train adjusters to use early statements against comparative fault arguments later.

## 2. Austin-Specific Information You Must Know

Austin’s growth has outpaced road design on several corridors. Knowing **where** crashes cluster and **which hospitals** serve trauma patients helps you make faster decisions.

### 2.1 High-Risk Corridors and Intersections (Educational Overview)

Crash data from TxDOT and local reporting consistently highlight stress points including:

- **I-35** through Central Austin (including the “Upper Deck” segment and frontage-road merges near downtown and UT)
- **MoPac (Loop 1)**—especially the express lanes and merges near Bee Caves Road and Far West Blvd
- **US 183** (Research Blvd / 183A corridor in Northwest Austin and Cedar Park approaches)
- **US 290** (Ben White Blvd / Oak Hill approaches and the Y at Oak Hill)
- **SH 71** to the airport and **SH 130** toll segments used as I-35 bypass routes
- **I-35 & US 290** interchange complexity and **I-35 & 71** near the airport
- Local arterials: **Lamar Blvd**, **Burnet Rd**, **Slaughter Ln**, **Parmer Ln**, and **Martin Luther King Jr Blvd** near UT

Construction zones—common on I-35 expansion projects—shift lane markings weekly. If your crash occurred adjacent to barrels and concrete barriers, photograph the **construction zone signage** and contractor information when posted.

### 2.2 Hospitals and Emergency Care in the Austin Area

For serious trauma, minutes matter. Major facilities Austinites commonly use after crashes include:

- **Dell Seton Medical Center at The University of Texas** (downtown, Level I trauma center—successor to the historic Brackenridge campus role in central Austin trauma care)
- **St. David’s Medical Center** (central Austin, including emergency services)
- **St. David’s North Austin Medical Center** (North Austin / Arboretum area)
- **Ascension Seton Medical Center Austin** (central)
- **St. David’s South Austin Medical Center** (South Austin)
- **Dell Children’s Medical Center** (pediatric emergencies)
- **Ascension Seton Northwest** and other suburban campuses for urgent needs in Northwest Austin

Urgent care can be appropriate for minor soft-tissue complaints, but **red-flag symptoms**—severe neck pain with numbness, repeated vomiting, worsening headache, chest pain, difficulty breathing—warrant ER evaluation. If you are unsure, call **911**.

Keep all facility names and dates consistent across insurance forms and medical records. Mixing “Brackenridge” colloquial language with Dell Seton billing names confuses adjusters; use the name on your discharge paperwork.

### 2.3 Texas Insurance Rules That Affect Austin Claims

Texas is an **at-fault insurance state**. There is no broad mandatory PIP requirement like some no-fault states, though many policies offer **Personal Injury Protection (PIP)** and **Medical Payments (MedPay)** as optional coverages. If you purchased PIP or MedPay, it may pay certain medical bills quickly regardless of fault—subject to policy limits and terms.

**Minimum liability limits** for Texas auto policies (as commonly cited in 2025–2026 educational materials) are often summarized as **30/60/25**: $30,000 bodily injury per person, $60,000 per accident, and $25,000 property damage. Many Austin drivers carry higher limits, but minimum policies still appear—especially in hit-and-run or underinsured scenarios where **UM/UIM** (uninsured/underinsured motorist) coverage on your own policy becomes critical.

Texas uses **modified comparative fault** (often called “proportionate responsibility”). If you are found **more than 50%** at fault, you generally cannot recover damages from other parties. If you are 50% or less at fault, your recovery may be reduced by your percentage of fault. Insurers exploit this framework aggressively in intersection and merge cases on MoPac and 183.

You must carry proof of financial responsibility. After a crash, verify the other driver’s insurance card and consider whether the vehicle might be a commercial or employer-owned unit with separate coverage.

### 2.4 Statute of Limitations and Deadlines

For many **personal injury** claims arising from negligence in Texas, the limitations period is commonly **two years** from the date of injury. Certain claims—wrongful death, government entity involvement, uninsured motorist contractual claims—may have **different deadlines**. If a **city of Austin** vehicle or **CapMetro** bus is involved, **government notice requirements** can be much shorter than two years.

Missing a deadline is usually fatal to a claim. This is one reason Austin residents consult attorneys **months before** the window closes—not the week before.

### 2.5 Police Reports and TxDOT Crash Reporting

When police respond, obtain the **Crash Report (CR-3)** identification details. You can often purchase reports through **TxDOT’s Crash Report Online Purchase System** once filed. If police do not respond and damage or injury thresholds are met, you may need to file a **Driver’s Crash Report (CR-2)** with TxDOT within statutory timeframes—verify current rules on TxDOT’s website or with an attorney.

Reports are not perfect. Officers may not list every witness or may misstate lane positions on complex I-35 merges. Your photos and independent witness statements still matter.

### 2.6 Educational Settlement Ranges (Not a Promise)

Austin-area injury claim values vary enormously based on medical bills, lost wages, permanency, insurance limits, and fault percentage. Educational discussions in 2025–2026 often cite **wide ranges**:

- **Minor soft-tissue** cases with limited treatment: may resolve in the **low thousands to low five figures** when liability is clear and limits are available
- **Moderate injury** with months of treatment and wage loss: **mid five figures to low six figures** in favorable scenarios
- **Severe injury** with surgery, permanent impairment, or high earners with large wage loss: **six figures and above**, sometimes policy-limited

These ranges are **illustrative only**. They are not quotes for your case. Insurers use software and prior settlements; juries in Travis County and surrounding counties have their own tendencies. No ethical referral service should guarantee an outcome.

## 3. How to Deal with Insurance Companies After an Austin Crash

Within hours, you may receive calls from the at-fault driver’s insurer and your own. Adjusters are trained professionals; you are allowed to be careful.

### 3.1 The Other Driver’s Insurance (Third-Party Claim)

The adverse carrier may request a **recorded statement** quickly. You are generally not legally obligated to give one on their timeline, though cooperation can be nuanced—especially under your own policy contract if you later make a UM claim. Many Texas attorneys advise accident victims to consult counsel **before** a recorded statement when injuries are significant or fault is disputed.

Provide a **written summary** when possible: date, location, vehicles, and that you are treating. Do not speculate about speed, distances, or traffic lights unless you are certain.

### 3.2 Your Own Insurance: PIP, MedPay, Collision, UM/UIM

Your declarations page lists coverages. **Collision** handles your vehicle subject to deductible. **Rental reimbursement** may apply with limits. **UM/UIM** protects you when the other driver is uninsured, underinsured, or a hit-and-run—common fears on late-night I-35 and 183.

If you use your own collision coverage for speed, your insurer may pursue **subrogation** against the at-fault party. Understand how subrogation affects you if you also settle bodily injury separately.

### 3.3 Property Damage and Total Loss in the Austin Market

Austin’s repair costs and rental car demand can delay repairs. Obtain **two estimates** if disputed. For total losses, insurers calculate actual cash value—market conditions after storms and chip shortages still affect valuations.

Document **aftermarket equipment** (lift kits, rims, work racks) with receipts. Gap insurance matters if you owe more than ACV on a financed vehicle.

### 3.4 Medical Bills and Liens

As bills arrive from Dell Seton, St. David’s, imaging centers on Duval or in Cedar Park, and chiropractors or PT clinics, upload them to your claim file. Some providers bill health insurance first; some use letters of protection in injury cases—an attorney can explain options.

**Medicare, Medicaid, VA, and employer ERISA plans** may have reimbursement rights. Ignoring liens can sink a settlement later.

### 3.5 When the Adjuster Offers a Quick Check

Early **“nuisance” settlements** for a few hundred or a few thousand dollars may require signing a **full release** of all claims. If you are still in treatment, signing global releases is dangerous. Read every paragraph. If pain continues after you cash a check, you may have waived rights.

### 3.6 Red Flags and Bad-Faith Patterns (Educational)

Watch for: excessive delays without explanation, ignoring documented medical records, misrepresenting policy language, or pressuring signature while you are on pain medication. Texas has insurance practices statutes and Department of Insurance complaint pathways; an attorney can advise on specific conduct.

## 4. Common Injuries & What to Expect

Austin crashes produce the same injury patterns seen nationwide, with local flavor from highway speeds and mixed traffic with scooters and cyclists.

### 4.1 Whiplash and Cervical Strain

Rear-end collisions on stop-and-go MoPac and I-35 frontage roads frequently cause **whiplash**. Symptoms—neck stiffness, headaches, shoulder pain—may peak 24–72 hours later. Imaging is not always ordered initially; follow clinician guidance. Physical therapy is common but should be medically directed.

### 4.2 Concussions and Traumatic Brain Injury

Airbag deployment, head strikes on window glass, or sports-style brain injury in younger drivers can cause **concussion**. Watch for brain fog, light sensitivity, sleep disruption, and mood changes. **Return-to-work** decisions matter in Austin’s tech and service economies; document cognitive limitations if your job requires screen time or driving.

### 4.3 Back, Spinal, and Disc Injuries

Higher-speed impacts on 130 or 183 may cause **herniated discs** or compression injuries. MRI timing is medical, not legal—but insurers scrutinize gaps in care. Keep appointments.

### 4.4 Fractures, Lacerations, and Airbag Injuries

Chest wall bruising, wrist fractures from bracing, and facial lacerations from airbags occur. Emergency departments stabilize; follow-up with orthopedics or plastic surgery may be needed.

### 4.5 Psychological Injuries

Anxiety driving on I-35 again, sleep disturbance, or PTSD symptoms are real. Mental health treatment is medical care; stigma should not block treatment.

### 4.6 Long-Term Recovery and Documentation

Create a folder (digital and physical) with:

- Every medical visit note and bill
- Pharmacy receipts
- Mileage to appointments (Round Rock specialists are common for suburban residents)
- Employer wage statements and PTO used
- Photos of bruising or scars over time

Consistency beats drama. Fact-based records outperform emotional social media posts insurers may find.

## 7. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ Schema Ready)

### Do I have to call the police after a car accident in Austin?

Not every crash requires police, but you should call **911** when anyone is hurt, vehicles block traffic, the other driver leaves, or you suspect intoxication. Texas reporting rules also may require a **CR-2** driver report to TxDOT in certain property-damage scenarios when police do not investigate. When unsure, call 911 and let dispatch guide you. This is general education—not legal advice for your crash.

### How long do I have to sue after a car accident in Austin, Texas?

Many **personal injury** claims based on negligence face a **two-year statute of limitations** in Texas, measured from the injury date. Wrongful death, government claims, and contractual UM disputes may differ—sometimes dramatically shorter. Missing a deadline can end your rights. Consult a licensed Texas attorney promptly if you think you may have a claim.

### What is Texas modified comparative fault and how does it affect my Austin case?

Texas applies **proportionate responsibility**. If you are found **more than 50%** at fault, you generally cannot recover from other at-fault parties. If you are **50% or less** at fault, recoverable damages may be reduced by your fault percentage. Insurers argue fault aggressively in merge and intersection crashes on MoPac, 183, and I-35 frontage roads.

### What are Texas minimum auto insurance limits?

Texas law sets minimum liability limits often summarized as **30/60/25** (bodily injury per person, per accident, and property damage). Many drivers carry more. Minimums may not cover serious Dell Seton-level trauma bills—which is why **UM/UIM** coverage on your own policy matters.

### Should I give a recorded statement to the other driver’s insurance company?

You may be asked soon after the crash. Many people consult a **licensed attorney** before giving a recorded statement when injuries are significant or fault is disputed. Misstatements can affect comparative fault determinations. You can often provide basic facts in writing instead. Educational only—not a rule for your specific policy.

### Do I need a lawyer for a minor Austin fender-bender?

Many property-only claims resolve without attorneys. If injuries are significant, treatment is ongoing, liability is contested, or a release would waive unknown future damages, a **free consultation** with a Texas injury attorney may be worthwhile. WreckMatch LLC can help you explore that option—we are not a law firm.

### Which Austin hospitals treat car accident injuries?

Major emergency and trauma resources include **Dell Seton Medical Center** (Level I trauma center downtown), **St. David’s** campuses (including North and South Austin), **Ascension Seton** facilities, and **Dell Children’s** for pediatric patients. Choose ER for serious symptoms; urgent care may suit minor complaints when appropriate.

### What if my accident happened on I-35 or MoPac during rush hour?

Document location with exit numbers and photos of signage. Secondary collision risk is high—prioritize lane safety and 911. Police may take longer during peak congestion; still file reports as required. Insurers know these corridors; detailed evidence helps.

### How do I get my Austin police crash report?

Request details at the scene. Once filed, purchase or access the **CR-3** through **TxDOT’s crash report system** or follow APD instructions on their website. Keep the report number in your insurance file.

### Does PIP or MedPay apply in Texas?

Texas does not mandate PIP like some no-fault states, but many policies include **optional PIP or MedPay**. If purchased, it may pay certain medical expenses per policy terms, sometimes regardless of fault. Read your declarations page or ask your agent—wording varies.

### What if the other driver was uninsured or fled the scene?

Uninsured and hit-and-run cases may trigger **UM/UIM** coverage on your policy if you purchased it. There may also be crime victim or investigation avenues through police. Prompt reporting matters. An attorney can explain options based on your policy language.

### Can I recover lost wages if I miss work after an Austin crash?

Document employer letters, pay stubs, and PTO used. Wage loss can be part of a bodily injury claim when liability and causation support it. Self-employed claimants need tax returns and contracts showing lost income. Educational ranges vary widely.

### What should I avoid posting on social media after a crash?

Avoid photos of you exercising, traveling, or commenting “I’m fine” if you later claim injury. Insurers monitor public profiles. Do not discuss fault or settlement online. Privacy settings are not guaranteed protection.

### How much is my Austin car accident case worth?

No honest referral service can promise a number. Value depends on injuries, treatment, permanency, insurance limits, fault percentage, and whether a jury is involved. Early adjuster offers are often low. Compare offers to complete medical picture before signing releases.
